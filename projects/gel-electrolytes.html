// Project 01 · Yang Lab, Columbia

Gel polymer
electrolytes

2026 —Ongoing

Question

Does an initially uniform electrolyte stay uniform inside a battery? Electrode surfaces, separators, and polymers may change the local electrolyte composition — and with it, how ions are solvated and how they move. The challenge is telling real composition differences apart from artifacts of sample preparation and measurement.

Approach

  • Quantitative ¹H/¹⁹F NMR — I built a workflow with sample-specific relaxation measurements to avoid relaxation-induced quantification bias, then compared neat electrolyte, equilibrated liquid, and gel extract, as well as cathode, separator, and anode extracts from multilayer pouch cells.
  • Raman depth series through stacked separators toward an NMC cathode, with custom fitting of overlapping bands and checks on baseline sensitivity.
  • Differential scanning calorimetry down to −80 °C to follow phase transitions.
  • Low-temperature impedance spectroscopy to compare ionic conductivity of liquid and gel electrolytes.

What I’ve found so far

  • A reproducible composition gradient toward the cathode has not been established yet — separator overlap and baseline sensitivity limit what the Raman spectra can assign.
  • Gel curing itself shifts solvent composition and salt-anion ratios. Directly heated gels differed most from their surrounding liquid, so I refined the curing protocol to reduce changes that would confound partitioning measurements.
  • Gelation lowered the DSC transition peaks (by 2.9 °C on cooling and 8.4 °C on heating), yet the liquid electrolyte’s conductivity was equal or higher at nearly all temperatures. Lower transition peaks alone don’t guarantee better low-temperature transport.
  • I also traced contact-resistance and cell-fabrication artifacts that can distort conductivity comparisons.

Status & next steps

Poster at the Columbia Undergraduate Research Symposium (October 2026); manuscript in preparation. Next, I’m helping set up Raman measurements of concentration polarization under applied current, and measuring the composition of mixed phases for a related phase-diagram project.

Advisor: Prof. Yuan Yang. Collaborators: Juncheng Lai and Zherui Xiao (Raman under applied current), Xiaohan Chen (phase-diagram project).
