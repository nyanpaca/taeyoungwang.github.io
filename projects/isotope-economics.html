// Project 03 · Columbia

Isotope
economics

2025Modeling

Question

Is it worth making aerospace materials out of their lightest stable isotope? Enriching a structural element in its lightest isotope makes it slightly less dense — and every kilogram saved in flight is valuable. But isotope separation is expensive. This project weighs the two against each other, element by element.

Approach

  • Implemented a techno-economic model that computes the per-element economic benefit of replacing structural aerospace materials with light-isotope-enriched versions.
  • Calculated enrichment cost from separative work unit (SWU) analysis of multi-stage separation cascades.
  • Derived cost-per-SWU scaling factors for gas centrifugation and chemical exchange, benchmarked against ²³⁵U and deuterium.

Key results

For each element, I optimized the enrichment level and stripping-stage ratio to maximize economic benefit while constraining feed-material consumption.

Status

Co-authored manuscript submitted (2026): “Economic Analysis of Light Isotope-enriched Elements for Aerospace Applications.”
