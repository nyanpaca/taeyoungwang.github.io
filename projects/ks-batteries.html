K–S batteries

2025 —Ongoing

Question

Can a room-temperature potassium–sulfur battery store energy for long durations? Potassium and sulfur are abundant and cheap. Pairing a K-β″-alumina solid electrolyte with a liquid polysulfide catholyte could make a flow-style battery suited to grid storage — if the polysulfides dissolve well enough.

Approach

  • Assembled and tested symmetric and full CR2032 coin cells with β″-alumina solid electrolytes.
  • Prepared K–Na alloy anodes and polysulfide catholytes in formamide and TEGDME.
  • Measured potassium-polysulfide solubility across candidate solvents by stepwise titration.
  • Built and ran a semi-flow prototype with a circulating formamide catholyte.
  • Calculated theoretical energy density and evaluated levelized cost of storage and installation costs.

Key results

  • Formamide stood out as a high-solubility catholyte solvent, dissolving K₂S up to 2.75 mol kg⁻¹.
  • The semi-flow prototype cycled for more than 70 cycles and over 400 hours.
  • Cost and energy-density estimates framed the system’s potential for long-duration energy storage.

Status

Co-authored manuscript submitted (2026): “Room-temperature K-S Flow Battery Enabled by K₂S₂/K₂S-soluble Formamide Electrolyte for Long-duration Energy Storage.”
